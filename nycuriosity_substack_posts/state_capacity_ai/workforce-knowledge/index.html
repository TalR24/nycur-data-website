More Workers Near the Exit

State capacity is ultimately people. The public workforce is stretched thin and aging, losing institutional knowledge as experienced staff retire faster than new hires arrive. The chart below shows the imbalance at the federal level: how many workers are near the exit versus just entering.

Why it matters: Just 7% of the federal workforce is under 30, while about 15% is already eligible to retire, more than twice as many. When those workers leave, decades of undocumented institutional knowledge leaves with them. Locally, NYC has roughly 19,000 funded but vacant positions, a 5.89% vacancy rate.

Where AI helps: Copilots that draft memos and answer routine policy questions, tools that capture a departing employee's knowledge before it walks out the door, and faster onboarding all build capacity directly. These tools work as augmentation; treating them as a path to layoffs would degrade the very expertise they depend on.

Examples to follow

  • Civil-servant copilots — assistants that help staff draft memos, navigate internal policy, and answer routine questions without pulling a senior colleague off their own work.
  • Knowledge capture — making a retiring employee's accumulated know-how searchable before their last day.
  • Onboarding assistants — letting new hires ask "how do we do X here" and get an accurate, sourced answer.
  • Rote-task automation — offloading data entry, case summarization, and scheduling that drive burnout and turnover.

Age structure: About 7% of the federal workforce is under age 30; roughly 15% is eligible to retire. Sources: Partnership for Public Service / OMB (2022); retirement-eligibility share via OMB and FEDweek.

NYC vacancies: The Citizens Budget Commission counts nearly 19,000 vacant full-time positions with budget available to fill them; the city's vacancy rate was 5.89% in 2025, down from a COVID-era peak above 8%. Sources: Citizens Budget Commission; City & State NY, 2025.

Note: The under-30 and retirement-eligible groups are not mutually exclusive of the rest of the workforce; they are two ends of the same age distribution.
