311 Noise Spikes vs. MCB3 Resolutions

When 311 noise complaints explode, does MCB3 respond with a formal resolution? And if so, how long does it take? This chart overlays monthly 311 "Noise – Street/Sidewalk" complaint counts in Community District 3 with the dates of corresponding MCB3 board resolutions, revealing a consistent pattern: the board lags residents by 1 to 13 months.

Methodology & Data Notes

311 data: Complaint counts are queried from the NYC Open Data 311 Service Requests dataset (dataset ID: erm2-nwe9), filtered to community board 12 MANHATTAN (Manhattan Community District 3) and complaint type Noise - Street/Sidewalk. Counts are aggregated by calendar month.

Complaint type selection: "Noise – Street/Sidewalk" was selected because it showed the sharpest pandemic-era spike with the most direct policy connection. The June 2020 peak of 7,314 complaints represents a 21× increase from the January–February 2020 baseline of ~340/month. "Illegal Fireworks" showed a higher spike ratio (213×) but had no corresponding MCB3 resolutions and was a shorter-lived phenomenon.

Resolution identification: MCB3 full-board resolutions were searched for keywords related to noise, open streets, outdoor dining, and quality of life. Resolutions were matched to the spike by relevance of text, not automated keyword matching. Three distinct resolutions were found across the 2020–2021 response window. Resolution dates are the full-board meeting dates from MCB3 meeting records.

Lag calculation: Lag is measured from the spike peak month (June 2020) to the resolution date. The July 2021 resolution is also compared against the 2021 secondary peak (June–July 2021: ~3,300–3,600/month).
