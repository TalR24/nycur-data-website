The Same Benefit, Delivered Unequally

SNAP is one federal program with one set of eligibility rules. Yet the share of eligible people who actually receive it ranges from barely half to everyone. The chart below shows each state's take-up rate: where a state lands is a measure of its delivery capacity, not its need.

Why it matters: Ten states reach 100% of their eligible population; Wyoming reaches just 55%. New York sits at 89%, above the 82% national average. The benefit and the rules are identical, so the gap is pure delivery capacity: outreach, application design, and how easy a state makes it to enroll and stay enrolled.

Where AI helps: Multilingual navigators, document processing to clear backlogs, and caseworker copilots can lift take-up in the states that lag, while a human stays in the loop on every denial. Americans spend an estimated 10.5 billion hours a year on federal paperwork; cutting that "time tax" is how low-uptake states catch up.

Examples to follow

  • Code for America, GetCalFresh, cut California's online SNAP application to under ten minutes and helped millions enroll; the team now builds AI-assisted intake and document tools.
  • Nava PBC, modernizes Medicaid, Medicare, and benefits systems for federal and state agencies, including document and eligibility processing.
  • Multilingual benefits navigators, chat assistants that answer eligibility questions overnight in a resident's first language, reducing call-center load.
  • Caseworker copilots, surface the relevant policy clause mid-call and draft plain-language denial and renewal notices that cut avoidable churn.
