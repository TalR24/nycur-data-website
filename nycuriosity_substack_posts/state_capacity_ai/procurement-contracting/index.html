Contracts Arrive Late, and Later Every Year

How government buys services is one of the deepest sources of dysfunction, and NYC's is getting worse. The city registers the contracts that fund shelters, food programs, and senior centers long after the work starts, forcing providers to float payroll on credit. The chart below tracks the share of contract value registered late, by category, and every line is climbing.

Download CSV (members)

Why it matters: By FY2024, 77% of all NYC contract value and 85% of nonprofit contract value was registered after the start date, up sharply from FY2021 in every category. Providers deliver the service first and wait months to be paid, often borrowing to cover payroll. A single broken back-office process ripples out to every resident those organizations serve.

Where AI helps: Drafting and standardizing RFPs, summarizing and comparing bids against stated criteria, and flagging duplicate contracts and improper payments can cut weeks from each step. Award decisions stay with human evaluators and must remain auditable and challengeable.

Examples to follow

  • AI-drafted solicitations — generating a first-draft RFP and scope of work from a plain-language description of what an agency needs.
  • Bid review copilots — summarizing and scoring vendor proposals against criteria so evaluation committees spend time on judgment, not page-turning.
  • Improper-payment detection — scanning spending data across agencies to surface duplicate contracts and waste, an established use at the federal level.
  • Vendor onboarding assistants — simplifying registration, the step that disproportionately screens out small and minority-owned businesses.

Metric: "Percent of late-registered contracts by value" is the share of contract dollars registered after the contract start date (retroactive registration), by fiscal year. Values: All Procurements 52.55% / 52.10% / 65.94% / 77.37%; Human Services 55.44% / 62.15% / 72.24% / 73.90%; Non-Profit 70.13% / 76.42% / 78.47% / 85.28% for FY2021 through FY2024.

Source: NYC Comptroller, Annual Summary Contracts Report (Chart 4). Figures are by contract value, not by count; a separate by-count measure of late registration runs higher.
