Ninety-Four Years at the Subway's Crossroads

West 4th Street–Washington Square opened in 1932 with an entire lower level built for a line that would not exist for another eight years. This timeline traces the station from that bet on the future through 27 years without express tracks, a failed first generation of elevators, and the renewal completed in May 2026.

Sources: Opening dates and service history from contemporaneous New York Times coverage (Sept 10, 1932; Aug 22, 1932; Dec 16, 1940; Apr 19, 1961) as compiled in the station's Wikipedia article. Name origin from the New York Times "F.Y.I." column (Sept 14, 2008); tile-color anecdote from Arthur Warner, "The City's New Underground Province," New York Times, Nov 22, 1931. Elevator failure figures from the New York Times ("$1 Billion Later, New York's Subway Elevators Still Fail," May 19, 2008) and WNYC (Oct 28, 2005). National Register listing from the National Archives (registration documentation, July 2004). 2025–2026 escalator and elevator completions from MTA contractor announcements (Forte Construction, J-Track/Entuitive).

Note on artwork: The "Greenwich Village Murals" mosaics often associated with this station are at Christopher Street–Stonewall on the 1 train, not West 4th Street.
