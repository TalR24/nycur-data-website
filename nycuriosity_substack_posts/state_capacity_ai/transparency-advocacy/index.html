The Comprehension Gap

Government is hard to watch from the outside and harder to understand. Open data is technical, regulations are written in legal jargon, and processes span agencies with no single map. The chart below shows the other half of the problem: how many adults can comfortably read dense official text in the first place.

Why it matters: More than one in four US adults now reads at Level 1 or below, where pulling a single piece of information from a dense document is difficult. That share rose from 19% in 2017 to 28% in 2023. Most government notices, regulations, and benefit letters are written well above this level.

Where AI helps: Plain-language translation, visual process maps, and document explainers let anyone interrogate a city charter, a regulation, or an open-data portal in their own words. The risk is hallucination, so outputs must cite and link the primary source rather than stand in for it.

Examples to follow

  • Vital City — Josh Greenman and team publish AI-assisted visual explainers, including a step-by-step map of how a NYC building permit moves through the system. See their work on AI, accountability, and discretion.
  • Block Party — NYCuriosity's own tool, increasing awareness and engagement of NYC community board meetings and resolutions.
  • Legislative document explorers — projects that turn New York City Council and US Congress transcripts into searchable summaries the public can track.
  • "Ask the charter" agents — assistants grounded in a specific city charter, state constitution, or open-data portal that answer plain-language questions with citations.

Literacy levels: From the 2023 Program for the International Assessment of Adult Competencies (PIAAC). 28% of US adults scored at Level 1 or below, 29% at Level 2, and 44% at Level 3 or above. The Level 1-or-below share rose from 19% in 2017. Source: National Center for Education Statistics, PIAAC 2023 National Results.

Reading-level note: Level descriptions are simplified for a general audience. Level 1 or below indicates difficulty with tasks such as locating a single piece of information in a longer text.
