The Benefit Take-Up Gap

Eligible residents routinely fail to receive benefits they qualify for. Application backlogs, college-level eligibility rules, and English-only forms create what researchers call the "time tax." The chart below shows the share of eligible people who never get three of the most common benefits, the gap AI navigators and document processing aim to close.

Why it matters: A benefit only works if eligible people actually receive it. Roughly one in five eligible Americans misses out on the EITC, and in New York City more than one in five appears eligible for SNAP but does not get it. The friction is administrative, not a lack of need.

Where AI helps: Americans spend an estimated 10.5 billion hours a year on federal paperwork (GAO, 2023). Multilingual navigators, document processing, and caseworker copilots can shrink that time tax, while a human stays in the loop on every denial.

Examples to follow

  • Code for America, GetCalFresh — cut California's online SNAP application to under ten minutes and helped millions enroll; the team now builds AI-assisted intake and document tools.
  • Nava PBC — modernizes Medicaid, Medicare, and benefits systems for federal and state agencies, including document and eligibility processing.
  • Multilingual benefits navigators — chat assistants that answer eligibility questions overnight in a resident's first language, reducing call-center load.
  • Caseworker copilots — surface the relevant policy clause mid-call instead of forcing a transfer, and draft plain-language denial and renewal notices that cut avoidable churn.

EITC: The IRS estimates 19.2% of eligible taxpayers did not claim the Earned Income Tax Credit for tax year 2022. Source: IRS EITC Central.

SNAP: Nationally, about 18% of eligible individuals did not participate in SNAP (USDA FNS, 2019). In NYC, 78.6% of apparently eligible residents received SNAP in 2024, leaving a 21.4% gap. Source: NYC HRA, SNAP participation, 2024.

Paperwork burden: 10.5 billion hours figure from GAO-25-107239 (federal information-collection burden, 2023). "Time tax" framing from the OIRA 2023 Burden Reduction Report.

Figures are drawn from different years and geographies and are labeled accordingly; they are not a single matched dataset.
