- COVERAGE STEP (do this first, it drives recall): list every sentence in NEW matter (braced text, or the whole text when a section is wholly new) that contains shall, must, may, is authorized, is empowered, is directed, will, or shall not. Each such sentence either produces at least one record or is listed in "excluded_sentences" with its first six words and a one-word reason from: private, entitlement, condition, exemption, definition, finding, appropriation, boilerplate, reprinted, duplicate. A sentence that names a government actor and a verb never goes unlisted. Long acts, special-district acts and appropriation bills still get every governance duty (meetings, elections, budgets, reports, bonds, audits) and every power recorded one by one.
- "authorized and directed", "empowered and directed", "directed and authorized" and "will <verb>" with a government subject are DUTIES, never powers.
- A sentence whose subject is the beneficiary or the amount ("eligible units shall receive an abatement", "interest shall be allowed", "the salary shall be increased", "members shall be credited with", "shall not be required to pay") is an entitlement, not a government duty: no record. A government body that must pay, issue, certify or compute something is still an actor when the sentence names it as the one acting.
- A condition on another provision is not a record: "shall not be implemented/paid/effective until X delivers a certificate", "subject to the approval of the director of the budget", "with the approval of", "approved by". The approver is never the actor of the underlying duty; record the underlying duty for the body that holds it, and record a separate approval power ONLY when the sentence itself grants the approval in operative words ("the director may approve or disapprove").
- When a sentence gives a duty or power to two or more bodies jointly ("the director of the budget and the director of employee relations may"), record one record per body, same quote.
- Parallel sections for different named persons, places, funds or amounts (Tier V grants to Mason, Strizzi, Horton; three appropriation directives) are separate records, one each, never collapsed.
- The actor is the body the law names as acting. "The borough president", "the board of elections", "the principal", "the board of education" in a New York City law resolve to the New York City body; "a public body" (Open Meetings Law) covers State and local bodies; "an approved assessing unit" is a city or town, not its county; "the authority" under the Alcoholic Beverage Control Law is the State Liquor Authority. A recipient of State aid ("surviving municipalities shall be awarded") is not the actor; the paying State body is.
- The quote MUST contain braced new matter when the law has braces. For an extension (a new sunset date, amount, place or class), quote the sentence that holds the changed words (the one with "{{2020}}"), never a neighbouring proviso or exemption, and never the amending instruction ("is amended to read as follows").
- A deadline stated as a recurring date ("on or before February first of each year") is kind fixed_date with the first such date on or after the act's effective date and the recurrence annual; "at each general election" or "prior to each election" is kind none; "shall be borne by" cost provisions are kind none, recurrence ongoing.
- The act clause ("This act shall take effect ...", "shall expire and be deemed repealed", "provided, however, that the amendments made by section ...") is boilerplate: list it in excluded_sentences with reason boilerplate, never a record. Effective and expiry dates are computed separately.
