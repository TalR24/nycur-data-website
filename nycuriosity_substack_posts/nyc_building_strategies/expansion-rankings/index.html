Ranking the MTA's Expansion Wishlist

Every candidate expansion project in the MTA's 2023 20-Year Needs Assessment Comparative Evaluation, ranked by its official cost-effectiveness metric: cost per minute of door-to-door travel time saved over 30 years (lower is better). Note the metric carefully — this is cost per minute saved, not cost per rider. The values span $0 to $262 per minute, so the five extreme outliers are shown separately below the main chart on their own compressed scale.

The IBX scored best of any proposed new line at $1.29 per minute saved — and it was the only project rated in the top category on 7 of 8 evaluation criteria. Three small projects technically scored better on the raw metric (the Ridgewood Busway and two junction/capacity fixes), but none of them is a new transit line. Among genuine expansion lines, the Second Ave Subway's crosstown extension to 125th St/Broadway came in second at $1.43/min.

The metric: "Cost/Time Saved (30 yrs)" is the MTA's own cost-effectiveness measure — the 30-year net present value of capital costs (construction plus fleet, in 2027 dollars) and operating & maintenance costs, divided by total door-to-door travel time saved. The MTA cautions that these are conceptual-level planning estimates, not rigorous cost estimates. The Ridgewood Busway shows $0/min because its projected operating savings over the project lifetime exceed its capital cost.

Ridership figures: The riders shown in each sublabel are total projected 2045 daily riders on the project, including riders diverted from existing MTA services — not net-new riders to the system. The gap can be large: the Second Ave Subway extension to Houston St projects 230,400 total daily riders but only 2,900 net-new.

Second Ave Subway Phase 2: Phase 2 proper (96th St to 125th St/Lexington) was excluded from the Comparative Evaluation because it was already committed and funded. The project evaluated here is the crosstown extension along 125th St to Broadway that would build beyond Phase 2.

IBX ridership vintages: The 118,700 figure shown is the 2023 evaluation's 2045 forecast. The MTA's current project page estimates 160,000 weekday riders — a later planning estimate, not the same forecast.

Excluded from the bars: Two LIRR infill stations (Elmhurst and Sunnyside) were evaluated but have no computed cost-effectiveness value because they produce no net travel time savings — they add stops that delay existing riders. They are in the downloadable CSV.

Sources: MTA 20-Year Needs Assessment, Appendix 06: Comparative Evaluation (2023); the full assessment is at future.mta.info. IBX current estimates from the MTA Interborough Express project page.
