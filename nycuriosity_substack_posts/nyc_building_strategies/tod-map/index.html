Where the Building Happens

Why these two projects belong on one map: the South of Prospect Plan is the first neighborhood rezoning drawn to plug directly into the IBX. Both corridors terminate at Avenue I, where the line's planned McDonald Avenue stop (F transfer) and East 16th Street stop (Q transfer, at Avenue H) would sit. The New York Building Congress estimates that rezoning around the full IBX corridor could support 70,000–100,000 housing units within a decade.

Station locations are preliminary: the 18 named station areas shown come from the MTA's January 2023 Planning and Environmental Linkages (PEL) study. The MTA and the Governor's office officially cite roughly 19 stations; 18 locations have been publicly named. Names, sites, and transfers may change during the environmental review that began in October 2025. Per-station subway letters are the author's mapping of named IBX stops to adjacent subway stations; the official 17-line connection count includes the B via the parallel Brighton corridor.

Corridors are approximate bands, not parcel lines: the South of Prospect Plan, announced May 20, 2026, covers McDonald Avenue from roughly Fort Hamilton Parkway to Avenue I and Coney Island Avenue from roughly Caton Avenue to Avenue I, plus surrounding blocks. Precise boundaries await the zoning concept expected in 2027, followed by ULURP. The city has not published a housing-unit projection; the Department of City Planning says the plan could create "potentially thousands" of units.

Ridership and cost: 160,000 projected weekday riders is the MTA's revised May 2025 estimate; the original PEL projection was 115,000. The ~$5.5B estimated cost includes $2.75B committed in the 2025–2029 MTA Capital Plan, per the Governor's August 2025 announcement. The 70,000–100,000 unit figure in the takeaway box is from the New York Building Congress 2025 IBX report and assumes rezoning around the full corridor.

Sources: Office of Governor Kathy Hochul, IBX announcement (Aug. 1, 2025); MTA, Interborough Express PEL Study (Jan. 2023); Gothamist (May 20, 2026); 6sqft (May 20, 2026).
