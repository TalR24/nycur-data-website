The two lanes move at very different speeds. All four completed transit megaprojects opened over an eight-year span (September 2015 to January 2023), and the next two are not expected until roughly 2030 and 2032. Housing reform compressed into 18 months: City of Yes (December 2024), the charter ballot proposals (November 2025), Block by Block (May 26, 2026), and the state's SEQRA overhaul (May 27, 2026) — the last two arriving one day apart.

Completed transit dates: the 7 train extension to 34 St–Hudson Yards opened September 13, 2015; Second Avenue Subway Phase 1 (72 St, 86 St, 96 St) opened January 1, 2017; Moynihan Train Hall opened January 1, 2021; Grand Central Madison opened January 25, 2023, with full LIRR service from February 27, 2023.

Target dates are targets, not actuals: Penn Station Access ($2.9B, four new East Bronx stations) was originally due in 2027; the MTA confirmed a roughly three-year delay in October 2025 and has not published a firm new opening date, so it is plotted as "target ~2030" (City & State NY; Engineering News-Record). Second Avenue Subway Phase 2's September 2032 revenue service target follows the $1.97B tunneling contract awarded in August 2025; the project's total cost is reported between $6.99B and $7.7B depending on the source.

Interborough Express: the IBX is plotted at its current phase, not an opening date — it entered preliminary engineering in August 2025 and environmental review in October 2025, and no construction start or opening date has been set.

Housing milestones: the City Council adopted City of Yes for Housing Opportunity on December 5, 2024, by a 31–20 vote (about 80,000 homes projected over 15 years). Voters approved charter Proposals 2, 3, and 4 on November 4, 2025, fast-tracking affordable housing and streamlining land use review. Mayor Mamdani released the Block by Block housing plan on May 26, 2026, targeting 200,000 new affordable homes built and 200,000 preserved over a decade (Mayor's office). Gov. Hochul signed SEQRA reform in the FY27 state budget on May 27, 2026, exempting qualifying housing — up to 500 units in higher-density NYC zones, 250 elsewhere in the city — from environmental review and capping EIS completion at two years (Greenberg Traurig).

Chart notes: Block by Block (May 26) and the SEQRA signing (May 27) fell one day apart; their markers are separated slightly so both stay legible. Milestones are positioned by month along the horizontal axis.
