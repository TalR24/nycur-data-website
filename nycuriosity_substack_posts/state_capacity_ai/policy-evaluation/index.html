The Evaluation Drop-Off

Government rarely checks whether its own laws work. A cost is projected before a bill passes, then almost never independently estimated, and almost never revisited against what actually happened. The chart below traces that drop-off across 244 recent NYC Council bills in NYCuriosity's Fiscal Impacts Tracker.

Why it matters: Every one of these 244 bills carries a projected cost, totaling about $8.45 billion in expenditure. But only 21 (8.6%) came with an independent OMB estimate, and none face a systematic retrospective review checking whether the projection held. We commit the money up front and look away.

Where AI helps: Synthesizing outcome data across agencies, scanning thousands of pages of audits and IBO reports, and tracking the gap between projected and realized impact. AI surfaces correlation, not causation, so its output is a lead to investigate, not a verdict.

Examples to follow

  • NYCuriosity Fiscal Impacts Tracker, our own tool making every NYC Council fiscal estimate legible and, over time, checkable against reality. Explore it.
  • Cross-agency outcome synthesis, pulling program results from siloed systems to estimate what is actually working.
  • Audit and report scanning, surfacing underperforming or redundant programs across thousands of pages of filings.
  • Natural-language data queries, letting a staffer ask a question of administrative data instead of commissioning a months-long study.

Counts: Of 244 NYC Council bills with a fiscal impact statement in the tracker, all carry a projected cost; 21 included an independent OMB cost estimate; none have a published retrospective evaluation. Source: author's analysis of NYC Council Legistar data via the NYCuriosity Fiscal Impacts Tracker, last updated June 2026.

Note: "Retrospective evaluation" means a published after-the-fact study comparing projected to actual fiscal impact. The absence of such reviews is the point; the tracker is one attempt to make the comparison possible.
