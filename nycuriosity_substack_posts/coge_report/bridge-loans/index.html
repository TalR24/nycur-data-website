The City Lent Nonprofits $104.6 Million Because It Had Not Paid Them

New York City runs a fund whose purpose is to lend human services providers the money the City itself owes them but cannot yet legally pay. The Returnable Grant Fund issued $104.6 million in interest-free loans across 305 loans in FY2025. It is not an emergency measure. It has operated at roughly this scale for most of the last seven years.

Download CSV (members)

A loan fund is the tell. Every dollar here is money the City has already committed to a provider under a contract, routed back to that provider as a loan because the payment machinery has not caught up. The fund spares nonprofits commercial interest. It does not spare them the administrative work, and it does nothing for providers who never apply.

Real fixes are moving, all of them outside the Charter. Local Law 156 of 2025 requires 50% of a human services contract's annual value within 30 days of registration. Local Law 11 of 2026 adds quarterly advance payments at homeless services and criminal justice. A discretionary grant pilot pays about 100 small nonprofits 100% upfront and cuts their wait from ten months to roughly six. None of it is on the November ballot, and none of it is in the Charter.

What is counted: the total value of loans issued from the Returnable Grant Fund in each fiscal year, as reported by the Mayor's Office of Contract Services. The fund provides interest-free bridge financing to human services providers awaiting contract processing and registration.

A quirk in the source: MOCS reports the same provider count (186) and loan count (305) for FY2024 and FY2025 alongside different loan values. The dollar values are charted; the counts are reproduced in the downloadable data as published.

Sources: Mayor’s Office of Contract Services, 2025 Citywide Indicators Report; COGE Adopted Final Report (July 23, 2026), Appendix.
