Nobody Measures Community Board Meetings. So We Started, With 820 of Them.

A prototype of the public dashboard recommended in NYCuriosity's community boards article: one place to see how every board is doing. This first cut covers Manhattan's 12 boards using two sources that already exist, the boards' own YouTube channels (meeting lengths, measured from 820 recent recordings) and Block Party's archive (records coverage). The metrics the article proposes that nobody currently collects, minutes timeliness, member attendance, and hybrid reliability, are exactly the gap this page makes visible.

What belongs on this dashboard that nobody collects: whether minutes are posted within the Open Meetings Law's two-week window, per-member attendance, whether a working hybrid option exists, and how many members of the public actually attend. Boards' own records contain all of it. The point of this prototype is that the measurable part took one afternoon; the missing columns are a policy choice, not a technical problem.

Every Manhattan board is on YouTube and in Block Party. All 12 boards publish recordings, and Block Party has processed 6,817 Manhattan meeting transcripts. The raw material for accountability metrics already exists; a borough president's office or the CEC could maintain this citywide.

Method: Author's analysis of the 12 Manhattan community board YouTube channels (videos and live streams tabs), collected July 2026. Sample = meeting-titled videos of at least 20 minutes, up to roughly the 80 most recent per channel; durations from YouTube metadata. Clips, promos, and non-meeting videos excluded by title filter. 108 videos (mostly CB11 and CB12) had restricted metadata and are excluded, which is why sample sizes differ. Full-board versus committee splits are approximate because boards title videos inconsistently.

Sources: the boards' public YouTube channels; Block Party archive transcript counts by board (July 2026). Related reading: Future of Community Boards Working Group report (2021) on board resourcing.
