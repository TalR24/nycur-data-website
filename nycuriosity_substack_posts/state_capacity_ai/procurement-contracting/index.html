How Late NYC Pays Its Nonprofits

How government buys services is one of the deepest sources of dysfunction. The clearest local symptom: NYC registers the human-services contracts that fund shelters, food programs, and senior centers long after the work has started, forcing nonprofits to float payroll on credit. The chart below shows how late, by delay bucket.

Why it matters: Only about one in nine human-services contracts is registered on time. In FY2023, 88.5% were late and nearly a third arrived more than a year after the contract start date; in FY2024 the late share rose to 90.7%. Nonprofits deliver the service first and wait to be paid, often borrowing to cover payroll in the meantime.

Where AI helps: Drafting and standardizing RFPs, summarizing and comparing bids against stated criteria, and flagging duplicate contracts and improper payments can cut weeks from each step. Award decisions stay with human evaluators and must remain auditable and challengeable.

Examples to follow

  • AI-drafted solicitations — generating a first-draft RFP and scope of work from a plain-language description of what an agency needs.
  • Bid review copilots — summarizing and scoring vendor proposals against criteria so evaluation committees spend time on judgment, not page-turning.
  • Improper-payment detection — scanning spending data across agencies to surface duplicate contracts and waste, an established use at the federal level.
  • Vendor onboarding assistants — simplifying registration, the step that disproportionately screens out small and minority-owned businesses.

FY2023 buckets: 11.5% of human-services contracts were registered on time; 27% between six months and a year late; 31% more than a year late; the remaining 30.5% less than six months late (the total late share was 88.5%). Source: NYC Comptroller, January 2024 report on FY2023 data.

FY2024: 90.7% of human-services contracts were registered late. Source: NYC Comptroller, February 2025.

On-time figure: The on-time share is computed as 100% minus the reported 88.5% late, for FY2023.
